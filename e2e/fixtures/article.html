Tidal Patterns of Marlow Harbour

By R. Pellworth

Marlow harbour experiences a semi-diurnal tide, with two high waters and two low waters in each lunar day. The mean spring range at the harbour mouth is 4.6 metres, one of the largest on this stretch of coast, and the mean neap range is a little under 2.1 metres.

The harbour bar, a sandbank that runs across the entrance, dries to 0.8 metres above chart datum at low water springs. Vessels drawing more than two metres therefore wait for the flood and cross the bar in the two hours either side of high water. The harbour master publishes a daily crossing window on the notice board by the fish quay.

Since 1952 the Marlow Maritime Society has kept a tide gauge on the north pier. Its records show the mean high water level rising by roughly 19 centimetres over seventy years, most of it after 1990. The society attributes the change to a combination of regional sea-level rise and the dredging of the outer channel in 1987.

A tidal race forms off the lighthouse headland during the ebb, running at up to three knots at springs. Small craft are advised to keep at least a quarter of a mile offshore until the race slackens, about one hour before low water.
